Methodology

How Property Check works.

The product separates property identity, source retrieval, normalisation and explanation. A failure at any stage remains visible rather than being turned into an apparent clearance.

1. Resolve the selected address

The user explicitly selects an address. The application preserves the address-to-lot-to-parcel chain and does not silently choose the first search result.

2. Retrieve source records

Jurisdiction-specific adapters query cadastral, planning, flood, heritage and constraint sources. Each response retains retrieval state and source limitations.

3. Normalise without over-interpreting

Results use neutral states: source fact, detected, not detected, unresolved, not assessed or unavailable. “Not detected” is never translated into “safe”.

4. Display the evidence

Every metric can expose its official owner, dataset, date, plain-English summary, limitations and representative raw record.

What comes later

Buyer relevance, cost exposure and any Lemon Metric should only be added after the source layer is robust across a diverse property set and the scoring method is calibrated.

Responsible use

What this site cannot prove.

It is not a title search

Mapped parcel and secondary-interest records are screening information. Legal ownership, encumbrances and easements require the appropriate paid title and conveyancing searches.

It is not a planning certificate

Displayed planning records do not determine whether a proposed use or renovation is permissible. A planner or certifier may need to assess the actual proposal.

It is not a flood or insurance clearance

Parcel flags do not prove that a dwelling floor is affected, nor whether insurance is available or affordable. Obtain the official report and a property-specific insurer quote.

It is not a building inspection or valuation

The site does not assess physical condition, identify defects, estimate value or recommend whether someone should buy the property.
